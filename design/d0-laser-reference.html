Cutting an opening in an I-beam web

Technical reference for the laser keyframes. Every render in the design boards has to agree with these three views. Not to scale; no machine parameters are given or implied.

Rules every laser keyframe must follow

  1. The beam lies with the web horizontal and the flanges vertical. The section stays fixed and the head moves.
  2. The nozzle and head body stay between the flanges and never intersect one, in any camera angle.
  3. Piercing comes first, inside the material to be removed. A short lead-in joins the contour, which then runs closed.
  4. The cut front stays localized at the kerf: a narrow orange-to-white point with a short, thin trail. No flame, no full-plate glow, no spark shower that hides the process.
  5. Any visible beam is captioned “Beam shown for illustration”.
  6. The removed part appears only in a labelled exploded or explanatory view. It never lifts itself out of the beam.
  7. No values: no laser power, speed, gas type, pressure, thickness or kerf width on any frame.
  8. Plasma, if shown, is a separate process with its own torch. The laser nozzle never morphs into a plasma torch.
  9. Every render carries “Illustrative render”. The beam and opening are generic, not a project part.

Sources used for plausibility

Machine configuration (head moving along a fixed H-section, multi-axis head for web and flange features): Bodor profile cutting line, JQLASER HL1250S gantry beam station, Weihong five-axis H-beam system.

Process physics (local melting, assist gas expelling melt through the kerf): Laser Cutting: A Review on the Influence of Assist Gas, Materials 2019, TRUMPF laser cutting overview.
